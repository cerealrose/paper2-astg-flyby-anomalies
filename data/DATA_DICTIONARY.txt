DATA DICTIONARY: flyby_data.csv
================================================================================

This file reproduces Table I of Nesvinga, Nyambuya & Jones (2026b),
"Earth-Flyby Velocity Anomalies within the Azimuthally Symmetric Theory of
Gravitation", in machine-readable form.

COLUMNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
spacecraft        Mission name.
date              Date of closest approach (perigee), ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD).
h_p_km            Perigee altitude above the reference geoid, km.
V_inf_kms         Hyperbolic excess speed, km/s. Blank for Rosetta II/III
                  (unpublished).
dVp_mms           Perigee velocity anomaly, mm/s. For the four anomalous
                  flybys and the two control flybys (Galileo II,
                  MESSENGER), this is DERIVED from Anderson et al. (2008)
                  Table I's dV_infinity via the vis-viva scaling
                  dVp = (V_infinity/V_p) * dV_infinity -- Anderson (2008)
                  does not report dVp directly. For Rosetta II/III, 0.0
                  denotes "consistent with zero" (their own words), not a
                  precise measurement. For Juno, 0.200 is the published
                  UPPER LIMIT (< 0.200 mm/s), not a central value.
sigma_mms         1-sigma uncertainty on dVp, mm/s, derived by the same
                  vis-viva scaling applied to Anderson (2008)'s
                  sigma(dV_infinity). Blank where not applicable
                  (Rosetta II/III).
delta_in_deg      Inbound (approach) geocentric declination of the
                  spacecraft's osculating velocity vector, degrees.
delta_out_deg     Outbound (departure) geocentric declination, degrees.
anomalous         True for the four flybys with a statistically
                  significant velocity anomaly (Galileo I, NEAR, Cassini,
                  Rosetta I); False otherwise.
source            Primary literature source(s) for this row.

PRIMARY SOURCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anderson2008    J. D. Anderson, J. K. Campbell, J. E. Ekelund, J. Ellis,
                and J. F. Jordan, "Anomalous orbital-energy changes
                observed during spacecraft flybys of Earth," Physical
                Review Letters 100, 091102 (2008). Table I. The
                foundational source for Galileo I, Galileo II, NEAR,
                Cassini, Rosetta I, and MESSENGER: h_p, V_infinity,
                dV_infinity, sigma(dV_infinity), and both declinations.

Jouannic2015    B. Jouannic, R. Noomen, and J. A. A. van den IJssel,
                "The flyby anomaly: An investigation into potential
                causes," Proc. 25th International Symposium on Space
                Flight Dynamics (ISSFD), paper 153 (2015). Extends the
                inventory to twelve encounters, including Galileo II and
                Juno.

Antreasian1998  P. G. Antreasian and J. R. Guinn, "Investigations into the
                unexpected delta-V increase during the Earth gravity
                assist of Galileo and NEAR," AIAA/AAS Astrodynamics
                Specialist Conference, paper AIAA 98-4287 (1998).
                Independent confirmation of the Galileo and NEAR dVp
                values; source of the Juno upper limit as used here.

ESA_Ros90,      European Space Agency Rosetta Mission Status Reports
ESA_Ros124      No. 90 (2007) and No. 124 (2009). Perigee times and
                altitudes for Rosetta II and Rosetta III.

JPL Horizons    Declinations for Rosetta II and Rosetta III were
                independently verified against JPL Horizons ephemerides
                (see the manuscript, Sec. III.D); agreement with the
                literature values used here is within 1% for every flyby
                with published data.

NOTES ON DATA PROVENANCE AND CORRECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An earlier secondary source's transcription of Anderson (2008) had two
errors, both corrected in this file and traceable via
scripts/03_anderson2008_data_correction.py:

  * Cassini's V_infinity was given as 1.601 km/s (a decimal-point error;
    the correct value, self-consistent with Cassini's own r_p and
    eccentricity via the standard hyperbolic-orbit relation, is
    16.010 km/s).
  * Cassini's dVp and Galileo I's sigma were each off by a factor of
    several, traced to the same secondary source rather than to
    Anderson (2008) itself.

Every value in this file has been independently re-derived directly from
Anderson (2008)'s own published Table I; see the verification script for
the full row-by-row reproduction.
